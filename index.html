1 · The future system: choose by meaning

will · be going to · Present Continuous · Present Simple

English has several ways to talk about the future.

The exam may offer more than one future-looking form, so the key is to identify why the speaker is talking about the future.

FormMain useExample
will + base verbinstant decision, promise, offer, general predictionI will call you tonight.
be going to + base verbprior plan/intention; prediction with present evidenceWe are going to study after class.
Present Continuouspersonal arrangement already organizedI am meeting Ana at 6.
Present Simplefixed schedule/timetableThe train leaves at 7:15.
Decision nowwill
Plan before nowgoing to
ArrangementPresent Continuous
TimetablePresent Simple
Common trap: Do not choose a future form only because the sentence contains tomorrow. The time word tells you when; the context tells you which form.
Exam strategy: Ask four questions: Was the decision made now? Was it planned before? Is it an arrangement with details? Is it a fixed schedule?

Quick Check · 3 items

1. A: The phone is ringing. B: I ___ it.
2. We ___ dinner with Carlos on Friday at 8:00. The reservation is confirmed.
3. The museum ___ at 9:00 tomorrow morning.

2 · be going to: plans and intentions

A decision already made before speaking

Use be going to for a prior intention or plan.

am / is / are + going to + base verb

The plan exists before the moment of speaking. It does not need to be fully arranged.

I am going to start a course next month.
They are going to move next year.
She isn't going to apply this semester.
Are you going to study tonight?

Plan vs arrangement

Going to emphasizes intention. The Present Continuous often suggests that details have already been organized.

I'm going to visit Cusco someday. → intention
I'm visiting Cusco on Friday. My ticket is booked. → arrangement
Common mistakes: I going to study ✗ · She is going study ✗ · Correct: I am going to study / She is going to study.
Exam strategy: Look for evidence that the decision already existed: I decided, I've planned, next month I intend to...

Quick Check · 3 items

1. We already decided. We ___ a new apartment next year.
2. She isn't ___ the course this term.
3. ___ you going to apply for the scholarship?

3 · be going to: prediction from present evidence

What you can see now points to the future

Use be going to when present evidence strongly suggests a future result.

The evidence is visible or already happening now.

Look at those clouds. It is going to rain.
Watch out! You are going to drop those boxes.
The team is playing terribly. They are going to lose.
The glass is at the edge. It is going to fall.

Evidence now

Going to is especially natural when the evidence is present and observable.

General prediction

Will is common when the prediction is based on opinion, belief or general expectation.

Common trap: I think it is going to rain can be possible in real English, but exam questions often contrast evidence-based going to with opinion-based will.
Exam strategy: If the sentence contains Look!, Watch out!, The sky is..., He is driving too fast..., check whether current evidence is pointing to a future result.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. Look at the sky! It ___ soon.
2. That child is running toward the wet floor. He ___ .
3. The car is making a terrible noise. It ___ down.

4 · will: decisions, promises, offers and predictions

A flexible future form with several functions

Will often reacts to the present moment.

FunctionExample
Instant decisionI'm tired. I will take a taxi.
OfferI will help you with those bags.
PromiseI won't tell anyone.
Prediction / opinionI think the exam will be difficult.

Forms

will + base verb · won't + base verb · Will + subject + base verb?
She will call later.
We won't forget.
Will you need help?
I think prices will rise.
Common mistakes: will to go ✗ · will goes ✗ · Correct: will go.
Exam strategy: Words such as I think, probably, I'm sure, I promise often support will. For an immediate response to a new situation, will is usually the best choice.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. A: I forgot my pen. B: Don't worry. I ___ you one.
2. I promise I ___ anyone your secret.
3. I think technology ___ even more important in education.

5 · Present Continuous & Present Simple for future meaning

Arrangements vs schedules

Present Continuous = personal arrangement

Use it when a future event has been arranged, especially when details such as time, place or another person are already fixed.

I am meeting my advisor at 4:00.
We are flying to Arequipa on Saturday.
She isn't working tomorrow.
Are you seeing the dentist this week?

Present Simple = timetable / fixed schedule

Use it for transport, classes, events, opening times and other fixed schedules.

The flight leaves at 6:20.
The class starts at 7:00.
The store closes at 9.
What time does the movie begin?
Common trap: A scheduled train is not your personal arrangement. Compare: The train leaves at 7. vs I'm taking the 7:00 train.
Exam strategy: Ask who controls the event. Personal arrangement → Present Continuous. Official timetable → Present Simple.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. We ___ our teacher after class tomorrow; the meeting is confirmed.
2. The bus ___ at 6:30 every morning.
3. I ___ the 8:15 flight on Monday. My ticket is booked.

6 · Future time clauses

when · before · after · as soon as · until

Use Present Simple in the future time clause.

When a time clause refers to the future, English normally uses a present form after when, before, after, as soon as, until—not will.

future main clause + when/before/after/as soon as/until + Present Simple
I'll call you when I arrive.
We'll eat after she gets home.
I'll tell you as soon as I know.
I won't leave until he comes.
Common mistake: I'll call you when I will arrive. ✗ → I'll call you when I arrive.
Exam strategy: If when/before/after/as soon as/until introduces a future time clause, check for a present form inside that clause.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. I'll text you when I ___ home.
2. We won't start until everyone ___ .
3. She'll send the file as soon as she ___ it.

7 · unless, until, as soon as & by the time

Small words, big meaning differences

These connectors are common exam distractors.

ConnectorMeaningExample
unlessif notWe won't go unless it stops raining.
untilup to the time whenI'll wait until you finish.
as soon asimmediately whenI'll call as soon as I arrive.
by the timeno later than the moment whenBy the time you arrive, I will have finished.

Unless = if ... not

Unless you study means if you do not study. Avoid adding another negative unless the meaning genuinely requires it.

You won't pass unless you study.
= You won't pass if you don't study.
Common trap: unless you don't study often creates the opposite meaning. Also remember: these clauses normally use Present Simple for future reference.
Exam strategy: Translate the logic, not the word. Ask: Does the sentence mean if not, up to that time, immediately when, or before that deadline?

Quick Check · 3 items

1. You won't improve ___ you practice regularly.
2. I'll stay here ___ you come back.
3. ___ you arrive, call me immediately.

8 · Future probability

likely · unlikely · could · probably

Future predictions can show different degrees of certainty.

ExpressionPatternExample
likelybe likely to + verbPrices are likely to rise.
unlikelybe unlikely to + verbHe is unlikely to come.
couldcould + base verbThe situation could improve.
probablywill probably + verbShe will probably pass.

Word order matters.

She will probably arrive late.
She probably won't arrive early.
It is likely to happen.
It could happen.
Common mistakes: is likely happen ✗ · will likely to happen ✗ in basic exam patterns → use is likely to happen or will probably happen.
Exam strategy: Check both meaning and structure. Likely/unlikely need be + adjective + to-infinitive. Could takes the base verb directly.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. The new policy is ___ to reduce costs.
2. She ___ finish early if the meeting is short.
3. They are unlikely ___ before noon.

9 · Future Continuous

will be + -ing

Use the Future Continuous for an action in progress at a future time.

will be + verb-ing
At 8 p.m., I will be studying.
This time tomorrow, we will be flying to Cusco.
Don't call at 10; she will be presenting.
Will you be using the car tonight?

Expected future activity

The Future Continuous can also describe something expected to be happening as part of a normal course of events.

I'll be working from home tomorrow.
They'll be staying at the hotel all week.
Common mistakes: will be study ✗ · will studying ✗ · Correct: will be studying.
Exam strategy: Look for a future point that answers the question What will be in progress then? Signals include at 8 tomorrow, this time next week, all afternoon.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. This time tomorrow, we ___ to Arequipa.
2. At 9 tonight, I ___ for the exam.
3. Don't call her at 3. She ___ a presentation.

10 · Future Perfect & the key contrast

will have + past participle vs will be + -ing

Future Perfect = completed before a future point.

will have + past participle
By Friday, I will have finished the report.
By 2030, the company will have opened five offices.
By the time you arrive, we will have eaten.
She won't have completed it by noon.

Future Continuous vs Future Perfect

QuestionFormExample
What will be in progress at that time?will be + -ingAt 6, I will be working.
What will be completed before that time?will have + ppBy 6, I will have finished.
byby thenby Fridayby the timebefore + future point
Common trap: At 6 often points to an activity in progress; by 6 creates a deadline and often points to completion.
Exam strategy: Circle at or by. Then ask: ongoing at that moment → Future Continuous; completed before that moment → Future Perfect.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. By next Friday, we ___ the project.
2. At 6 p.m. tomorrow, she ___ .
3. By the time the class starts, I ___ the chapter.
Week 6 exam habit: Do not choose a future form from the time expression alone. First identify the meaning: instant decision, prior plan, arrangement, timetable, future time clause, probability, action in progress, or action completed before a deadline.
